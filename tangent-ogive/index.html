// 01 — Overview

Problem statement

The tangent ogive is one of the most common forebody shapes in high-speed aerodynamics, used in everything from projectiles to launch vehicles. Its curved profile weakens the bow shock relative to blunt geometries, reducing wave drag at supersonic and hypersonic speeds.

This study evaluates how well Newtonian impact theory—a simple analytical model—predicts forebody pressure drag compared to full CFD. An axisymmetric tangent ogive with a short cylindrical afterbody is analyzed at zero angle of attack and Mach 5, comparing Newtonian theory against inviscid Euler and viscous RANS simulations.

// 02 — Methods

Three approaches

01
Newtonian Impact Theory
Analytical model assuming surface pressure depends only on local surface inclination angle. Both pure Newtonian ($C_{p,\max}=2$) and modified Newtonian ($C_{p,\max}=1.6$) variants were computed and validated against the Mayo tables.
Analytical Python Mayo Tables
02
Inviscid Euler CFD
Compressible Euler equations solved on three progressively refined meshes (4,458 to 64,677 cells) using a 2nd-order upwind flux scheme with gradient limiting. Grid convergence study performed.
Star-CCM+ Coupled Flow Axisymmetric Grid Study
03
Viscous RANS (SST)
Compressible RANS with Menter's $k$–$\omega$ SST turbulence model on a 260,287-cell mesh. 2nd-order upwind convection, adaptive CFL, and a wall-resolved boundary layer with growth ratio of 1.2.
Star-CCM+ SST Coupled Flow 260K cells
Newtonian pressure coefficient
$$C_p(\theta) = C_{p,\max}\,\sin^2\!\theta$$
Modified Newtonian stagnation pressure
$$C_{p,\max}(M_\infty, \gamma) = \frac{2}{\gamma M_\infty^2}\left[\frac{2\gamma M_\infty^2 - (\gamma-1)}{\gamma+1} - 1\right] = 1.6$$

The forebody pressure drag was computed by integrating axial pressure over the ogive surface with reference area $S_{\text{ref}} = \pi R^2 = 11.34\,\text{m}^2$.

Euler mesh refinement

// 04 — Conclusions

What this means

  • Newtonian theory matches Euler CFD — predicts the forebody pressure drag of a Mach 5 tangent ogive to within a few percent of converged inviscid results ($C_{D,p} \approx 0.313$), demonstrating excellent performance for rapid preliminary analysis.
  • Euler confirms Newtonian assumptions — grid-converged inviscid results show that numerical errors are small compared to physical modeling differences for this geometry and flow regime.
  • Viscous effects add ~9–10% — RANS yields higher pressure drag ($C_{D,p} = 0.342$), highlighting the role of shock–boundary-layer interaction even when skin-friction drag itself is negligible.
  • Practical implication — for early-stage hypersonic design of slender bodies at zero angle of attack, Newtonian theory provides a fast and reliable estimate. Euler or RANS should be reserved for higher-fidelity validation or viscosity-driven flow physics.
